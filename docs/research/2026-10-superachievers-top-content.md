# What Top Peer Groups Are Doing Right Now to Attract and Keep Members

*Research as of September 30, 2026*

This report covers what the highest-performing peer groups, masterminds, and family-office networks are doing to attract and retain members through content and outreach. It is not about what they say they do. It is about what the public record shows is actually performing.

## 1. The Top Offerings

### Large Established Networks

#### YPO (Young Presidents' Organization)
- **Price**: $8,910 annual dues plus $12,650 one-time initiation, before local chapter fees and events ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))
- **Size**: Global network, 220+ chapters across 76 countries ([source](https://www.ypo.org/become-a-member/))
- **How people join**: Application-based. Must be CEO/President/Board Chair age 54 or under at a qualifying-size company ([source](https://www.ypo.org/become-a-member/))
- **Front-door message**: "To find out more about YPO membership, please tell us a little bit about yourself. YPO welcomes membership applications from women 54 and under who have reached the title of President, CEO Chairperson of the Board (or the equivalent)." ([source](https://www.ypo.org/become-a-member/))

#### EO (Entrepreneurs' Organization)
- **Price**: $4,400–$7,000 annual all-in ($2,630 global dues + chapter dues), plus $3,500 initiation fee (member-reported) ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))
- **Size**: 18,000+ members, 220+ chapters, 76 countries; South Asia alone has 3,000+ members and a 99% renewal rate ([source](https://eonetwork.org/blog/10-standout-eo-moments-in-2025-focused-on-learning-leadership-and-community/))
- **How people join**: Must be a founder or owner past $1M annual revenue; application required ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))
- **Front-door message**: "Our community...has a global business network of over 18,000 influential business owners...Our mission is to drive both business and personal growth through peer-to-peer learning, providing support for a holistic entrepreneurial experience." ([source](https://podcasts.apple.com/au/podcast/the-eo-business-podcast/id1440350419))

#### Vistage
- **Price**: About $16,500 annual dues plus $2,500 initiation fee ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))
- **Size**: Described as "the default pick for established mid-market CEOs" with local groups of 12–16 ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))
- **How people join**: Application for established small and mid-market CEOs (revenue usually $5M+) ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))
- **Front-door message**: "Question. Challenge. Contribute." — emphasis on peer advisory with Chair-led structure and one-to-one coaching ([source](https://vistage.co.uk/research-centre/business-leadership/vistage-on-the-hill-question-challenge-contribute/))

#### TIGER 21
- **Price**: $34,000 annual (North America), $5,000 initiation; adjusted annually by CPI ([source](https://tiger21.com/faq_category/membership/))
- **Size**: 700+ members managing $70+ billion in personal assets ([source](https://www.successstorypodcast.com/timothy-daniels-president-and-ceo-of-tiger-21-the-membership-club-for-billionaires/)); groups meet monthly
- **How people join**: Invitation-only; must show $20M+ in verifiable investable assets or net worth; extensive vetting process ([source](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/top-family-office-associations-and-networks-you-should-know-about/))
- **Front-door message**: "Once you reach a certain level of success, it can be difficult to know where to turn to talk through what's really on your mind...increasingly, UHNW individuals are looking to peer advisory groups" to discuss "wealth preservation, family dynamics, investment portfolios, and what the next chapter might look like." ([source](https://tiger21.com/insights/a-personal-board-of-advisors-the-value-of-peer-insight/))

- **B2B newsletters**: ~31% open rate, 1.0–1.3% click-through rate (CTR), 3.4–4.1% click-to-open rate (CTOR) for media publishers ([source](https://www.omeda.com/resources/report/newsletter-benchmark-report-for-media-h1-2026/))

*Note*: Most elite peer groups do not publish their content engagement metrics. Where numbers are available above, they are included; where unavailable, that is stated plainly.

## 3. Patterns: What Drives Applications for Invitation-Only Groups

### What Actually Works

**Member-driven referrals**
- Multiple sources emphasize referral as the most credible path. Forum Select: "Referrals from existing members carry real weight. The member's credibility is on the line." ([source](https://openfutureforum.com/how-to-join))
- The Boardroom: "invite/referral-only membership...members have to be nominated and vetted" ([source](https://exa.ai/library/organization/byz0rcgczxt))
- Founders Network: "We'll match you with a member to serve as your nominator" ([source](https://foundersnetwork.com/request/))

**Specific, verifiable entry criteria**
- TIGER 21: "$20M+ in verifiable investable assets or net worth" ([source](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/top-family-office-associations-and-networks-you-should-know-about/))
- Hampton: "$3M+ revenue, or $3M raised, or $10M+ exit" ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))
- Cracks Mastermind: "$5M+ USD annual revenue" ([source](https://cracksmastermind.com/))
- EntreHouse Mexico City: "$2M+ annual revenue, verified" ([source](https://entrehouse.com/mexico))

**Proof of peer quality through real problems, not polish**
- Hampton member testimonial: "I feel comfortable both shooting the shit with and talking through the really ugly, messy parts" ([source](https://joinhampton.com/explore-membership))
- YPO LABx: "Members shared real stories of challenge, resilience and growth" creating "powerful connections built on trust" ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ypoglobal_ypoglc2026-leadershipinaction-betterleadersbetterworld-activity-7426913160240234496--iXn))
- TIGER 21: "Questions you can't ask friends, family, colleagues, or even advisors...opening up your entire portfolio—investments, income, expenses" ([source](https://tiger21.com/insights/a-personal-board-of-advisors-the-value-of-peer-insight/))

**Confidentiality as a feature, not fine print**
- Exit Five (B2B marketing VPs/CMOs): "Everyone signs an NDA upon joining. This has been huge because of the trust it creates. Execs in our community are sharing comp, equity, the CEO asking about negotiation, severance, having hard conversations with their team" ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_we-have-a-private-group-of-100-b2b-vps-and-activity-7482388377858691072-8-nx))
- IPI: "Safe harbor, non-solicitous environment" ([source](https://www.instituteforprivateinvestors.com/membership))
- Apex Inner Circle: "Rigorous qualification process to preserve the trust and high standards that define the community" ([source](https://www.apexgroup.com/insights/apex-group-launches-the-inner-circle-new-model-for-family-office-to-family-office-co-investment/))

**Scarcity that is real, not manufactured**
- EntreHouse Mexico City: "The first fifty decide who the next fifty are" ([source](https://entrehouse.com/mexico))
- Relationship Capital: "Spots are limited to 250 founding members for 2026" ([source](https://www.relationshipcapital.org/ourofferingsnav-1))
- Apex Inner Circle: "Membership is capped at 100 qualified family offices" ([source](https://www.apexgroup.com/insights/apex-group-launches-the-inner-circle-new-model-for-family-office-to-family-office-co-investment/))
- Proven Chaos Mastermind: "Ten seats. The right ten matters more than a fast ten." ([source](https://provenchaos.com/mastermind/))

**Member stories that show vulnerability, not victory**
- Mistakes Over Failure (YPO): "Missteps, breakthroughs and deep commitment to learning that CEOs experience while building and leading their businesses" ([source](https://www.ypo.org/2026/05/want-to-be-a-successful-leader-get-comfortable-with-contradictions/))
- TIGER 21 member content: Bonnie McGrath on "transitioning to a new identity & sense of purpose" after selling a business ([source](https://tiger21.com/news/after-selling-a-business-bonnie-mcgrath-podcast/))
- Hampton Moneywise: "Ultra wealthy people...reveal their personal finances...You couldn't get that info anywhere else on the internet" ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/parrsam_were-starting-a-hampton-youtube-channel-activity-7427008897854619648-59Hq))

**Content as trust-building, not lead capture**
- Hampton's product-first approach: "Many companies start with media then try to make a product. In most every case, they fail...Hampton built the product first, proved the value, and now content is just acquisition" ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/parrsam_the-entire-plan-with-hampton-i-love-content-activity-7470987740659134466-8t-E))
- Moneywise designed "to build the brand identity, without feeling too sales-y...content that was made to be valuable for them, the listener" ([source](https://lowerstreet.co/case-studies/hampton-moneywise))

**Repeatable participation loops**
- Podcasts work when they create "a repeatable weekly participation loop: one clear prompt, one low-friction response channel, and next-episode follow-through where audience input is featured" ([source](https://whatayarn.com/blog/podcast-audience-engagement-strategies-2026))
- Chief of Staff Network: "The podcast became the top of that funnel...The newsletter deepens that relationship...The 25+ local chapters...turn those digital relationships into real-world connections" ([source](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/the-chief-of-staff))

**Member-only research and data**
- Hampton: State of SaaS, Salary & Compensation reports using member data; "gated research to capture emails and nurture prospects" ([source](https://community.inc/article/hampton))
- FOX: Biennial Compensation & Benefits Study, Family Wealth Advisor Survey, all gated for members ([source](https://www.familyoffice.com/knowledge-center/research))
- TIGER 21: Asset allocation data, cryptocurrency polls, portfolio benchmarks shared among members ([source](https://www.successstorypodcast.com/timothy-daniels-president-and-ceo-of-tiger-21-the-membership-club-for-billionaires/))

### What Reads as Hype (Red Flags)

**One-size-fits-all frameworks pushed onto every member**
- "Many masterminds...facilitator pushed a single strategy or framework onto everyone in the group, regardless of industry, audience, or business model...leaves people feeling unseen and unsupported" ([source](https://www.jamiesaunderscoaching.com/why-most-masterminds-fail-and-how-i-do-it-differently/))

**Masterminds built on motivation instead of peer learning**
- "Many masterminds are built around hype instead of substance. There's a time and place for encouragement, but when business owners invest thousands of dollars and hours of their time, they're looking for more than just motivational quotes" ([source](https://www.jamiesaunderscoaching.com/why-most-masterminds-fail-and-how-i-do-it-differently/))

**Charismatic host becomes the product**
- "A mastermind's risk is that the host becomes the product. If the room depends on one charismatic operator, members may get doctrine instead of peer learning" ([source](https://phoenixforum.net/insights/ypo-vs-mastermind-which-room-fits-your-needs/))

**Recruitment-focused, not member-focused**
- "Conversation is about how to bring in new people into the system, showing screenshots, and nudging newcomers to buy traffic or upgrades" rather than "developing real skills" ([source](https://scambustersusa.com/olsp-system-review-2025-update/))

**Vanity metrics instead of ICP fit**
- "Which episode topic produced the highest-quality subscribers, not merely the most plays? A highly shared episode can be valuable, but a smaller episode that creates 100 engaged subscribers who later buy a product may be strategically more important." ([source](https://elma.sh/blog/newsletter-podcast-strategy))

**No structure or accountability**
- "Too many masterminds lack it. No clear agenda. No follow-up between meetings. No system for accountability. When there's no plan in place, people lose focus." ([source](https://www.jamiesaunderscoaching.com/why-most-masterminds-fail-and-how-i-do-it-differently/))

**Posturing instead of real problems**
- "If everyone is too advanced, members posture instead of sharing. If everyone is too early, the group becomes emotional support without traction." ([source](https://blog.mean.ceo/women-collaborations-vs-competition-revenue/))

## 4. What This Means for a Closed, Invitation-Only Group with No Ads

### The 5 Best Moves

**1. Make the room the product**
- Build trust and peer quality first. Do not launch publicly until you have proof that the five to seven people in the room genuinely help each other and would refer peers who belong. As EntreHouse says: "The room is the product. One wrong member costs everyone." ([source](https://provenchaos.com/mastermind/))

**2. Use specific, verifiable entry criteria**
- Do not rely on vague language like "successful founders" or "family principals who want to grow." State the floor clearly: revenue, assets, decision authority, or a comparable signal that people can self-assess. TIGER 21's "$20M+ in verifiable investable assets" and Hampton's "$3M+ revenue" work because qualified people know immediately whether they meet the bar. ([source](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/top-family-office-associations-and-networks-you-should-know-about/)) ([source](https://www.leadersadapt.com/ceo-peer-group-2025-guide/))

**3. Lead with member stories that show the messy middle, not the highlight reel**
- YPO's *Mistakes Over Failure* and TIGER 21's "life after a business sale" content work because they show what people actually struggle with: "What's next after an exit? How do I parent in the context of wealth? What do I do when my portfolio feels too concentrated?" Vulnerability is the signal that the room is safe. ([source](https://www.ypo.org/2026/05/want-to-be-a-successful-leader-get-comfortable-with-contradictions/)) ([source](https://tiger21.com/news/after-selling-a-business-bonnie-mcgrath-podcast/))

**4. Make referrals easy and explicit**
- The best new members come from current members. Make it simple: "Who else is working on a 90-day goal right now who would make this group better?" Forum Select's guidance is direct: "Ask them to refer you to [leader name] directly. Referrals from existing members carry real weight. The member's credibility is on the line." ([source](https://openfutureforum.com/how-to-join))

**5. Create a repeatable, low-friction way for potential members to see what you're about before they apply**
- Do not make people guess. Hampton's Moneywise podcast, Vistage's *Perspectives* magazine, and FOX's research reports all work because they give prospects a clear, recurring experience of the group's perspective and quality without requiring a sales call. For Superachievers, that could be a quarterly 90-day goal retrospective, a member story series, or a short research note. ([source](https://lowerstreet.co/case-studies/hampton-moneywise)) ([source](https://vistage.com/research-center/business-leadership/resilience-in-action-perspectives-2026/))

### The 5 Things to Avoid

**1. Do not start with content, then try to build the group**
- Hampton's founder is clear: "Many companies start with media then try to make a product. In most every case, they fail. Why? Because building a life changing product is much harder than doing media." Build the group first, prove the value, then use content to attract the next qualified cohort. ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/parrsam_the-entire-plan-with-hampton-i-love-content-activity-7470987740659134466-8t-E))

**2. Do not default to motivational language or empowerment framing**
- Hype repels the people you want. "Many masterminds are built around hype instead of substance...when business owners invest thousands of dollars and hours of their time, they're looking for more than just motivational quotes and surface-level feedback." ([source](https://www.jamiesaunderscoaching.com/why-most-masterminds-fail-and-how-i-do-it-differently)) Superachievers should sound like a working session, not a retreat.

**3. Do not make the founder or facilitator the star**
- If the group depends on one charismatic operator, you are building a personality brand, not a peer group. "If the room depends on one charismatic operator, members may get doctrine instead of peer learning." ([source](https://phoenixforum.net/insights/ypo-vs-mastermind-which-room-fits-your-needs/)) The members are the asset.

**4. Do not create artificial urgency or false scarcity**
- Real scarcity is "we only take people who meet the criteria and the room has to stay small to work." Fake scarcity is countdown timers and "only 3 spots left." The latter works for courses. It does not work for invitation-only peer groups. People see through it.

**5. Do not skip the vetting process to fill seats faster**
- "When masterminds aren't curated with intention, they can attract people with vastly different levels of experience, commitment, or even values. That can create friction and disengagement." ([source](https://www.jamiesaunderscoaching.com/why-most-masterminds-fail-and-how-i-do-it-differently)) One wrong member costs the whole group. Wait for the right person.
