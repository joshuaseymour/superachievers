# Superachievers Deep Research Report
*Research conducted October 2026*

## 1. Executive Summary

**Recommendation: Reshape and revive Superachievers in 2027, but with significant design changes.**

Key findings:

- **The core tension is real**: Peer groups succeed when members value judgment capital over information. The five to seven founder model matches proven forum sizes, but shared-goal accountability is structurally different from pure peer advice and carries higher dropout risk.

- **What actually holds groups together**: 90%+ would recommend membership in established peer networks like [EO (92% renewal)](https://eonetwork.org/newsroom/news-releases/entrepreneurs-organization-s-fy20232024-annual-report-highlights-strong-member-retention-and-further-investment-in-member-experience/), and [93% cite accountability as a reason to stay](https://digital.sandiego.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1935&context=dissertations). But [mastermind groups lose 10-25% annually](https://www.thesuccessalliance.com/blog/group-attrition-drop-out-mastermind/) and [over 90% of new groups fail entirely](https://www.lxcouncil.com/blog/why-mastermind-groups-fail). The difference: vetting, facilitation, clear non-transactional culture, and monthly (not quarterly) cadence.

- **Spouse/family participation works when structured carefully**: Family offices now [formalize spouse onboarding](https://thefopro.com/the-stewardship-roadmap-inside-one-large-sfos-engagement-cultivating-curriculum/) with staged orientations, family ambassadors, and clear participation pathways. Families that skip this face friction, especially around confidentiality and role clarity.

- **Legal structures exist but require counsel**: Co-ops, LLCs with operating agreements, and club deal SPVs all support surplus-sharing. [U.S. co-ops under Subchapter T can distribute patronage dividends](https://cooperatives.com/blog/cooperative-vs-llc) to avoid double taxation. [Mexico's Sociedad Cooperativa distributes surplus based on participation, not capital](https://www.expanship.com/mx/blog/types-of-companies-in-mexico), with mandatory 10-20% reserves. Neither structure is trivial; both need a lawyer.

- **90-day goal groups work differently than standing forums**: [Weekly accountability meetings with scorecards](https://monday.com/blog/productivity/12-week-year-time-management-system/) and [public or partner check-ins](https://more-value.com/90-day-sprint-framework/) drive execution. But [two misses in 90 days is a tight window](https://www.eosworldwide.com/rocks); EOS leadership teams use it for priorities, not peer bonding.

- **The biggest risk is mismatch**: If Superachievers attracts people who want a sounding board and gives them a production deadline, they churn. If it attracts people who want a sprint buddy and gives them process-heavy governance, they leave. Clarity up front is survival.

**Next step for Joshua**: Finish the 10 conversations, then decide: Is this a 90-day execution sprint with light peer support, or a trust-based peer group with optional shared projects? Do not try to be both at once in year one.

## 2. Why Small Peer Groups Hold Together or Collapse

### What the evidence shows

Established peer networks report strong retention when membership is curated and structure is consistent:

- [EO renewed 92% of members in FY2023/24](https://eonetwork.org/newsroom/news-releases/entrepreneurs-organization-s-fy20232024-annual-report-highlights-strong-member-retention-and-further-investment-in-member-experience/)
- [Across EPAGs (Executive Peer Advisory Groups), 95% would recommend membership and 93% cite accountability as a reason to stay](https://digital.sandiego.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1935&context=dissertations) *(University of San Diego dissertation on YPO, EO, and Vistage, survey data)*
- [Vistage members typically stay over five years](https://vistage.com/research-center/member-experience/top-ceo-networking-groups/)

But informal masterminds face steep attrition:

- [Typical mastermind programs lose 10-25% of members annually](https://www.thesuccessalliance.com/blog/group-attrition-drop-out-mastermind/)
- [Over 90% of new mastermind groups fail to launch or sustain](https://www.lxcouncil.com/blog/why-mastermind-groups-fail) *(LXCouncil industry observation, not peer-reviewed)*

### Size

Forum sizes across networks:

- [EPAG forums: 6 to 16 members, most commonly around 10-12](https://digital.sandiego.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1935&context=dissertations)
- [TIGER 21 groups: similar small cohorts](https://tiger21.com/tiger-21-family-office-groups/)
- [EOS Rocks teams: leadership groups of 3-7](https://www.eosworldwide.com/rocks)

Why this range: Large enough for diverse perspectives, small enough that everyone speaks at every meeting. Below five, one absence breaks quorum. Above fifteen, airtime dilutes.

### Cadence

Monthly is standard for peer advisory; weekly for execution accountability:

- [EPAG forums meet monthly](https://digital.sandiego.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1935&context=dissertations)
- [90-day sprint models use weekly check-ins (15-20 minutes) to review scorecards](https://monday.com/blog/productivity/12-week-year-time-management-system/)
- Quarterly is too infrequent for trust-building or real accountability

### Confidentiality

[Confidentiality is the cornerstone of trust](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/training-peer-mentors-facilitation-guide.pdf), but breaches happen:

- [34% of group leaders reported a member breaking confidentiality in a two-year period](https://egrove.olemiss.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1020&context=jcrp)
- [54% of experienced group leaders felt members had violated confidentiality at some point](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01609510902874586)

When confidentiality breaks, groups experience:
- Anger toward the violator
- Reduced self-disclosure
- Decline in cohesiveness
- Increased resistance
- [Sometimes member withdrawal](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01609510902874586)

What helps:
- [Read confidentiality rules at every meeting, even with no new members](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/178031585674457717-0230032020/render/PeerGroupFacilitatorGuide.pdf)
- [State limits clearly: harm to self or others, abuse, legal duty to report](https://www.mirecc.va.gov/visn1/docs/508CompliantProducts/PDFs/PartIIPeerSupportGroupFacilitationSkills.pdf)
- [Discuss consequences of violation before it happens](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01609510902874586) (76.7% of effective groups do this)
- Active facilitator response when breach occurs—passivity allows rupture to spread

Small communities have special risk: people may know each other outside the group, or family members may be in the same town. Superachievers with five to seven families in overlapping social circles faces this.

### Facilitation

[Over 90% of new mastermind groups fail](https://www.lxcouncil.com/blog/why-mastermind-groups-fail) largely due to poor facilitation:

- Letting dominant voices monopolize
- Not holding members to preparation or attendance
- Failing to intervene when group drifts off topic
- Unclear or inconsistent structure

Effective facilitators:
- [Use round-robin to ensure everyone speaks](https://ceal.sdsu.edu/_resources/files/peer-to-peer-support-facilitator-training-guide-updated.pdf)
- [Set time limits per person](https://ceal.sdsu.edu/_resources/files/peer-to-peer-support-facilitator-training-guide-updated.pdf)
- [Redirect side conversations](https://www.mirecc.va.gov/visn1/docs/508CompliantProducts/PDFs/PartIIPeerSupportGroupFacilitationSkills.pdf)
- [Call out off-track Rocks in the meeting, then IDS (Identify-Discuss-Solve) immediately](https://help.eos.ninety.io/en/articles/13639438-introduction-to-rocks)

### Member Selection

The TIGER 21 model is instructive:

- [Standard membership: $20M+ investable assets or net worth](https://tiger21.com/membership-qualifications/)
- [Family Office division: $200M+ family net worth](https://tiger21.com/tiger-21-family-office-groups/)
- [Vetted through "5Cs": Character, Conditions, Capacity, Contribution, Capital](https://tiger21.com/membership-qualifications/)
- [Meeting the financial threshold does not guarantee admission](https://tiger21.com/faq_category/membership/)—qualitative assessment of ethics, engagement, and fit is mandatory
- [$33,000/year + $5,000 initiation (Family Office: $50,000/year)](https://www.businessinsider.com/tiger-21-founder-wealth-preservation-network-2025-2)

Why this works: Self-selection (price) plus active vetting filters for serious, aligned members. Invitation-only with unanimous vote on new seats creates buy-in.

**Uncertainty note**: No published retention data for TIGER 21 or YPO specifically; testimonials and rankings suggest high satisfaction but are not independently verified.

## 3. What Members Actually Value and Pay For

They're curious about governance, next-generation engagement, hiring CEOs and CIOs, building family offices, compensation, direct investing, AI, philanthropy, and what has or hasn't worked in practice. The conversation is often less about 'what did you buy?' and more about 'how did you decide?'"

From [CCC Alliance members](https://www.cccalliance.com/testimonials):

Having the chance to speak with thoughtful, interested and creative peer families from around the country has been a wonderful resource as our family thinks strategically about our future."

> "I leave every meeting with pages full of notes that I reference often, pockets full of business cards that I don't throw away at the airport, a head full of ideas I can't wait to implement, and a heart full of renewed passion for continuing our family's legacy."

> "The CCC Alliance has been a wonderful resource and asset to my family and me. In fact, the network helped save my son's life!"

From [EO member reviews](https://vistage.com/research-center/member-experience/top-ceo-peer-groups/):

> "EO Accelerator has completely changed the way I look at my business."

> "EO members describe a community that is 'warm and welcoming, remarkably so,' and one that fundamentally changes how founders see their businesses."

From [YPO member reviews](https://vistage.com/research-center/member-experience/top-ceo-peer-groups/):

> "The most valuable professional community I belong to."

> "Unmatched for global connections."

> "The quality and caliber of fellow members... the confidentiality and trust within the forum."

*(However, some note that the forum experience varies significantly by chapter and local engagement levels.)*

### What the patterns show

Members value:

1. **Judgment capital, not just information**: ["Connection capital measures how many people you can call when the answers actually matter, not how many contacts you have"](https://share.snipd.com/episode/a0493a56-729c-4959-b5b4-853ccaf27bd9)

2. **Process wisdom over content**: How did you decide? How did you think through governance? What failed and why?

3. **Non-transactional relationships**: [TFOA (The Family Office Association) has "a strict code of conduct around privacy and confidentiality and no contact information or email lists will ever be shared. Members of the group are not actively marketing products or services to other members."](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sharpemarc)

4. **Accountability**: [93% cite accountability as a reason to stay](https://digital.sandiego.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1935&context=dissertations), but accountability for what varies—decisions, learning, showing up, or execution.

5. **Benchmarking**: ["About 90% reported they joined because, among other things, they wanted to have 'a clearer performance benchmark'"](https://digital.sandiego.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1935&context=dissertations)

6. **Safety to be vulnerable**: Groups succeed when members can admit uncertainty, mistakes, and fear without judgment.

Members do not primarily value:
- Keynote speakers
- Content libraries
- Conferences (though these add value as supplements)
- Advice from people who have not been in the seat

**Price as signal**: High fees self-select serious participants. [TIGER 21: $33,000-50,000/year](https://www.businessinsider.com/tiger-21-founder-wealth-preservation-network-2025-2). [Vistage, EO, YPO in similar range](https://tiger21.com/faq_category/membership/). The price is not for content; it is for access to the right peers and for the group's curation cost.

For Superachievers: If the value is peer judgment on life structure and goal design, price should reflect that and filter accordingly. If the value is co-creation and shared wins, price may matter less than skin in the game (deposits forfeit on dropout).

## 4. Shared-Goal or Co-Creation Groups Specifically

### What is different from pure peer advice forums

Co-creation requires:
- [A shared vision](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/helenbevanhealthcare_collaboration-co-creation-are-not-the-same-activity-7270036426677723136-2aAx)
- [Thinking in questions instead of problems and solutions](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/helenbevanhealthcare_collaboration-co-creation-are-not-the-same-activity-7270036426677723136-2aAx)
- [Creating psychological safety for people to open up](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/helenbevanhealthcare_collaboration-co-creation-are-not-the-same-activity-7270036426677723136-2aAx)
- [Making stuff and not just talking](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/helenbevanhealthcare_collaboration-co-creation-are-not-the-same-activity-7270036426677723136-2aAx)

This is structurally different from listening to a peer describe a challenge, offering perspective, and moving on.

### Real examples

**ARKI 135 Value Co-Creation Lab** ([case study](https://learnsy.fi/blog/reflection-on-the-arki-135-value-co-creation-lab)):
- Three working sessions + showcase, part of EU-funded circular economy project
- Case companies with real challenges + co-creators (designers, educators, digital innovators)
- ["What made this work was who was in the room... material side stream suppliers, potential upcyclers, and those connecting them across the ecosystem... That mix made the difference between abstract ideas and concrete ones."](https://learnsy.fi/blog/reflection-on-the-arki-135-value-co-creation-lab)
- ["The final showcase brought everyone together to share what they had learned. People noted that the lab had helped them build relationships that ordinary business encounters rarely allow."](https://learnsy.fi/blog/reflection-on-the-arki-135-value-co-creation-lab)

**McGill IMHL live consulting assignment** ([case study](https://cocreateconsultancy.com/case_study_cpt/mcgill-university-imhl-module-4/)):
- Senior health leaders worked directly with NHS leaders on "real, unresolved challenges"
- ["Groups met their clients, gathered information with no pre-existing knowledge of the issues, developed recommendations through design thinking and peer collaboration, and presented back to their clients in a formal session."](https://cocreateconsultancy.com/case_study_cpt/mcgill-university-imhl-module-4/)
- ["This created a genuine consulting experience that was unpredictable, sometimes uncomfortable and deeply formative."](https://cocreateconsultancy.com/case_study_cpt/mcgill-university-imhl-module-4/)
- Used Three Horizons framework, SystemCraft, Design Thinking, and Friendly Consulting
- [Described as "one of the most impactful learning experiences of the programme"](https://cocreateconsultancy.com/case_study_cpt/mcgill-university-imhl-module-4/)

**Study group that actually worked** ([case study](https://datafield.dev/how-to-learn-anything/part-05/chapter-31/case-study-01.html)):
- Five members, weekly, 7-9pm, 90-minute sessions
- First seven weeks: passive review (reading notes aloud, watching videos together)—unsatisfying
- After Week 7 exam results, one member brought research on collaborative learning
- Pivoted to: individual preparation required, quiz master prepares 3-5 hard questions, collaborative problem-solving, teach-back on tough concepts
- ["The retrieval practice revealed gaps. The collaborative discussion fixed them."](https://datafield.dev/how-to-learn-anything/part-05/chapter-31/case-study-01.html)
- ["Making the sessions more rigorous made them more enjoyable... 'We were actually learning,' says Tariq. 'That's more satisfying than sitting around pretending to learn.'"](https://datafield.dev/how-to-learn-anything/part-05/chapter-31/case-study-01.html)

**90 Days of Action cohort** ([case study](https://www.systemsdriven.co/articles/90-days-of-action)):
- Paid cohort, $1,500 skin in the game each
- Daily scorecards posted in Slack; someone hunts you down if you miss
- Weekly review: scorecard + blockers
- ["Solo, find one person, a friend or cofounder, and text them your scorecard daily. If you can't find anyone, the daily tracking alone still works."](https://www.systemsdriven.co/articles/90-days-of-action)

### What worked

1. **Real work, not hypotheticals**: When the task is a made-up exercise, engagement drops. When it is a real problem with stakes, people lean in.

2. **Clear deliverable and deadline**: Shared projects need an actual thing to make (a presentation, a prototype, a decision, a tested hypothesis).

3. **Individual accountability before group**: Study group case is clear—passive group review fails; individual preparation + group synthesis works. [Positive interdependence means "I need you to do your part for us to succeed together."](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/blogs.uoregon.edu/dist/9/17877/files/2023/02/Peer-Learning-Guide-V2.pdf)

4. **Structured process**: Co-creation frameworks (Three Horizons, Design Thinking, IDS) prevent drift.

5. **Reflection on what was learned**: [Groups succeed when they reflect on process: what worked, what didn't, what we'd change next time.](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/media/livacuk/centre-for-innovation-in-education/diy-guides/designing-peer-learning-groups-and-activities/designing-peer-learning-groups-and-activities.pdf)

### Failure modes

1. **Passive attendance**: If people show up but don't prepare or contribute, the group becomes a lecture or networking event.

2. **No consequences for non-delivery**: If someone commits and doesn't deliver, and the group shrugs, accountability collapses.

3. **Mismatched goals**: If some members want a sounding board and others want a sprint team, both leave disappointed.

4. **Vague outcomes**: "Let's collaborate on something" without defining what or when leads to diffusion and dropout.

5. **Too complex for 90 days**: If the shared goal requires coordination across five families' businesses and timelines, it is probably too ambitious for a first cycle.

**For Superachievers**: The one shared 90-day goal idea is elegant but fragile. If the goal is genuinely shared and scoped so each piece can succeed or fail independently, it might work. If one person's piece depends on another's delivery, a single dropout breaks the chain and the group collapses.

## 5. Spouse and Next-Generation Participation in Family Groups

### Evidence

[40% of family businesses are either currently undergoing or will undergo leadership succession within the next decade](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/services/deloitte-private/perspectives/family-business-succession-planning-next-generation.html) (Deloitte, 2026 survey of 1,587 family businesses).

[Only 37% of current leadership expresses high confidence in the next generation's preparedness.](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/services/deloitte-private/perspectives/family-business-succession-planning-next-generation.html)

[Next-gen members are most active in technology (51%), philanthropy (51%), innovation (49%), and sales/marketing (50%)](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/services/deloitte-private/perspectives/family-business-succession-planning-next-generation.html)—roles that help drive change, not legacy governance.

[70% of next-gen are already deeply engaged in family enterprise; 48% run significant internal operations](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2019/next-gen-survey-2019.html) (PwC 2019 survey of 950+ next-gen).

### Spouse integration: what works

Family offices increasingly formalize spouse onboarding:

**Three Hills Family Office** ([case](https://thefopro.com/the-stewardship-roadmap-inside-one-large-sfos-engagement-cultivating-curriculum/)):
- High-level booklet: family history, values, vision/mission; how the family thinks about wealth; overview of businesses and services; even a favorite recipe and photos
- New spouses invited to share their own family stories so the relationship feels mutual from day one
- Follow-up sessions: what to expect in family meetings, how to contribute, pathways for roles, practical guardrails for structures like discretionary trusts
- ["Clear pathways for married-in spouses to express interest, prepare for roles and step in with confidence"](https://thefopro.com/the-stewardship-roadmap-inside-one-large-sfos-engagement-cultivating-curriculum/)

**Two-stage meeting model** ([case](https://www.familybusinessmagazine.com/family-offices/bringing-new-family-members-into-the-family-office/)):
- First meeting for engaged couples: family culture, history, values/vision/mission, intro to how the family stewards capital
- Second meeting after marriage: details about family businesses, how to participate (board seats, foundation, family meetings)

**Family ambassador approach** ([FBCG](https://www.thefbcg.com/resource/uncommon-wisdom-on-welcoming-newcomers-to-enterprising-families/)):
- Assign a family member (not the spouse's partner) to mentor the new spouse
- ["The family ambassador can also serve a mentor by explaining important historical markers in the family and enterprise, sharing special traits about the enterprising family and discussing future opportunities for the new spouse's participation in family meetings."](https://www.thefbcg.com/resource/uncommon-wisdom-on-welcoming-newcomers-to-enterprising-families/)
- ["Starting slowly is important with lots of time for questions."](https://www.thefbcg.com/resource/uncommon-wisdom-on-welcoming-newcomers-to-enterprising-families/)

### What must be clear

1. **Are spouses allowed to work in the family business?** ["If a family does choose to tap into the talents of spouses—they should plan for and anticipate what would happen to the spouses' employment in the event of a divorce."](https://www.generation6.com/blog/significantothers)

2. **Do spouses participate in family meetings?** ["We feel that spouses should be a part of family meetings... The reality is that spouses will hear about what happens in the family meetings second hand in most cases. We feel that it is better that they have the ability to participate... and hear the information first hand."](https://www.generation6.com/blog/significantothers)

3. **What is confidential, and to whom?** Spouses inside the meeting are bound by group rules; spouses outside the meeting will hear secondhand. Be explicit.

### Pitfalls

1. **No onboarding**: ["Families seek engagement from new family members. But they often don't realize you can't expect engagement without understanding, and you can't get understanding without education."](https://www.familybusinessmagazine.com/engagement/spouses/onboarding-new-family-members-must-be-intentional-process/)

2. **Differential treatment**: If some spouses participate and others don't, resentment builds. If the policy is unclear, assumptions fill the gap.

3. **Forced participation**: ["A new family member may not want to participate in formal family meetings and this should be respected. Forcing someone to participate will likely lead to resentment."](https://www.thefbcg.com/resource/uncommon-wisdom-on-welcoming-newcomers-to-enterprising-families/)

4. **Throwing them into conflict**: ["Families who are experiencing powerful emotional conflict are advised to move slowly in welcoming a new spouse to formal meeting processes. A new marriage takes adjustment and throwing a spouse into an emotional mine field is not likely to produce feelings of engagement or appreciation for the family."](https://www.thefbcg.com/resource/uncommon-wisdom-on-welcoming-newcomers-to-enterprising-families/)

### For Superachievers

If Joshua plans to allow "someone from the family can sit in," clarify:
- Does that mean spouse, adult child, business partner, or all of the above?
- Do they observe or participate?
- Are they bound by the same confidentiality agreement?
- If a family sends different people each quarter, does that break continuity?
- If one member brings their COO and another brings their spouse, are those equivalent?

Structured onboarding and clear criteria prevent confusion and resentment. If the answer is "figure it out per member," write that in the agreement and expect friction.

## 6. Legal and Structure Options for Credit and Surplus-Sharing Among Members

**Disclaimer: This section describes options and flags complexity. It is not legal advice. Superachievers must consult counsel before implementing any structure.**

### Co-op structures

**United States:**

- [Cooperatives distribute surplus as patronage dividends in proportion to each member's transactions with the cooperative, not in proportion to capital contribution.](https://cooperatives.com/blog/cooperative-vs-llc)
- [Under Subchapter T, U.S. cooperatives can deduct qualifying patronage dividends paid to members from their taxable income. This means the cooperative pays corporate income tax only on retained earnings—the distributed surplus escapes double taxation.](https://cooperatives.com/blog/cooperative-vs-llc)
- [The member pays income tax on the patronage dividends received.](https://cooperatives.com/blog/cooperative-vs-llc)
- [Cooperatives typically raise capital through member equity, retained earnings, and member loans—not through stock sales.](https://ncbaclusa.coop/blog/differences-between-cooperatives-and-corporations/)
- [Some states (California, Minnesota, Colorado) allow hybrid structures that combine cooperative governance with LLC or corporation features.](https://cooperatives.com/blog/cooperative-vs-llc)

**Mexico:**

- [Sociedad Cooperativa (SC), governed by the Ley General de Sociedades Cooperativas (LGSC), enacted 1994, last amended 2009.](https://www.expanship.com/mx/blog/types-of-companies-in-mexico)
- [Cooperatives do not distribute profits as dividends but rather as surplus returns or yields (rendimientos).](https://www.expanship.com/mx/blog/types-of-companies-in-mexico)
- [Surpluses are determined annually by the General Assembly and distributed to members in proportion to their participation.](https://coops4dev.coop/sites/default/files/2021-03/Legal%20Framework%20Analysis%20-%20Mexico.pdf)
- [For production cooperatives, distribution is typically based on capital contribution; for consumer cooperatives, based on volume of goods acquired during the fiscal year.](https://coops4dev.coop/sites/default/files/2021-03/Legal%20Framework%20Analysis%20-%20Mexico.pdf)
- [Cooperatives must allocate 10-20% of annual surpluses to a mandatory reserve fund before any distribution occurs.](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LGSC.pdf) *(Ley General de Sociedades Cooperativas, Artículo 54)*
- [Minimum five members; one-member-one-vote regardless of capital.](https://www.expanship.com/mx/blog/types-of-companies-in-mexico)
- [Raising external investment capital is structurally difficult, since ownership cannot be transferred through shares in the conventional sense.](https://www.expanship.com/mx/blog/types-of-companies-in-mexico)

**Ask counsel:**
- Does a group of seven families taking on one shared goal quarterly count as "transactions with the cooperative" for patronage purposes?
- If surplus comes from a joint project (e.g., launching a product together), how is "participation" measured—time, capital, deliverables?
- Do annual distributions work if the group cycles quarterly?

### LLC with operating agreement

**United States:**

- [LLCs can be structured to mimic cooperative governance and patronage-based distributions through their operating agreement.](https://cooperatives.com/blog/cooperative-vs-llc)
- [LLCs taxed as partnerships must allocate all income to members annually](https://cdi.coop/coop-llc-retain-earnings/)—members owe income tax on their full share even if only part is distributed.
- [To retain earnings in an LLC taxed as a partnership, one member can be a separate subsidiary LLC whose express purpose is to retain earnings; that subsidiary is taxed as a corporation and pays tax on retained income.](https://cdi.coop/coop-llc-retain-earnings/)
- [If structured as an LLC without Subchapter T election, cannot deduct patronage dividends the way a co-op can.](https://cooperatives.com/blog/cooperative-vs-llc)
- [LLCs provide maximum flexibility: members can negotiate custom profit-sharing, voting, and management arrangements in the operating agreement.](https://ahclaw.com/benefit-post/differences-between-membership-in-a-cooperative-and-membership-in-a-limited-liability-company/)

**Mexico:**

- U.S. LLCs with Mexican members: ["U.S. LLCs with Mexican members may claim treaty benefits if fiscally transparent"](https://exa.ai/library/publication/4yp23y9hrwl) *(uncertain: verify treaty current as of 2027)*.
- Mexico has its own LLC-like entity (SRL, Sociedad de Responsabilidad Limitada), but it is typically capital-based and less flexible than the U.S. LLC for patronage-style distribution.

**Ask counsel:**
- If the group is U.S.-based with one Mexican member, does that trigger cross-border tax issues?
- If surplus is small or nonexistent in year one, is the LLC formation cost worth it, or should the group start with a simpler contractual agreement?
- How do distributions work if one member contributes capital and another contributes time?

### Club deal / Syndicate SPV model

- [Used by angel investors and small groups co-investing in one deal.](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/syndicate-agreements-in-the-uk-what-startup-investors-should-know/)
- [Typically formed as a Delaware LLC.](https://www.syndicately.com/blog/spv-operating-agreement-key-terms/)
- [Operating agreement defines contributions, manager authority, voting, profit waterfall, transfer restrictions, dissolution, and tax reporting.](https://www.syndicately.com/blog/spv-operating-agreement-key-terms/)
- [Profit waterfall: expenses/reserves → return of capital → optional preferred return → catch-up (if any) → residual split (e.g., 80% members / 20% lead carry).](https://www.allocations.com/insights/carried-interest-in-a-deal-spv)
- [Disclose setup and management fees separately from carry.](https://www.allocations.com/insights/carried-interest-in-a-deal-spv)
- [Securities counsel required for Rule 506(b) vs 506(c) compliance.](https://www.allocations.com/insights/club-deal-vs-syndicate-spv)
- ["A club deal is a small, already-known group writing one ticket; a syndicate SPV is a lead who sources the deal and brings followers."](https://www.allocations.com/insights/club-deal-vs-syndicate-spv)

**For Superachievers:**

This model is overkill unless the shared goal is a literal investment (joint venture, property purchase, startup co-investment). If the shared goal is "launch a product together" or "co-host an event," the SPV machinery is probably inappropriate.

**Ask counsel:**
- If the group invests time and small capital into a shared project and that project generates revenue, is that a security requiring 506 compliance?
- If surplus is split based on "participation," does that make it a partnership for tax purposes?

### ROSCA (Rotating Savings and Credit Association) model

- [A ROSCA is any savings club in which a group of participants makes regular contributions to a common pool, and one member receives the entire pool each cycle.](https://wsccouncil.org/savings-clubs/roscas)
- [Each member contributes the same amount at each meeting, and one member takes the whole sum once.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotating_savings_and_credit_association)
- [Order of distribution can be random, predetermined, or by auction (members who need capital sooner bid for early turns).](https://wsccouncil.org/savings-clubs/roscas)
- [ROSCAs are typically informal, private agreements based on social trust rather than formal banking.](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/community-development/discussion-papers/discussionpaper-ROSCAs.pdf)
- [Usually no records kept; no money retained inside the group.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotating_savings_and_credit_association)
- [ROSCAs are often legally distinct from formal co-ops.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772655X23000393)

**For Superachievers:**

If the deposit and surplus model is structured as "members contribute $X, and the pot rotates to whoever needs capital for a project or life event," that is a ROSCA. If surplus comes from shared work rather than contributions, it is not.

ROSCAs work when the contribution and payout are simple and mechanical. They fail when the model mixes social trust with complex outcomes or credit dependencies.

**Ask counsel:**
- Is a ROSCA subject to state banking or lending regulations?
- If surplus is not from contributions but from shared project revenue, is ROSCA structure even relevant?

### Summary table

| Structure | Surplus distribution | Tax treatment (U.S.) | Tax treatment (Mexico) | Formation complexity | Best for |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------------|----------------------|---------------------|----------|
| **U.S. Co-op** | Patronage-based | Subchapter T: deduct patronage dividends | N/A | Medium | Groups with clear transaction or participation measure, ongoing operations |
| **Mexico SC** | Participation-based, 10-20% reserve required | N/A | Entity-level tax; surplus distribution to members | Medium | Mexico-based groups, member-service focus |
| **U.S. LLC** | Custom (operating agreement) | Partnership tax (pass-through); or corporate if elected | Treaty benefits possible (uncertain) | Low to medium | Flexible, short-term experiments, small groups |
| **Delaware SPV** | Waterfall: capital return → pref return → carry | Partnership tax (pass-through) | N/A (U.S. entity) | High | Actual co-investment deals, not shared projects |
| **ROSCA** | Rotating pot | None (informal) | None (informal) | Very low | Fixed contributions, rotating disbursement, no project revenue |
| **Contractual agreement only** | Whatever members agree | Members report individually | Members report individually | Very low | No surplus in year one; test before formalizing |

### Recommendation for Joshua

**Year one: contractual agreement only.** No surplus to split yet; no need for entity formation. Simple written agreement:
- Members agree to confidentiality, showing up, finishing one's piece.
- If a project generates revenue, it goes into a reserve fund.
- At 12-month mark, group votes on whether to formalize and how to split reserve.
- Deposits refunded if group completes cycle; forfeit on dropout (unless group votes otherwise).

**If group continues past year one and surplus exists: U.S. LLC or Mexico SC, depending on where members are based.** Get counsel in both jurisdictions if cross-border.

**Do not use SPV unless the shared goal is an actual investment.**

**Do not assume ROSCA structure works if surplus comes from shared work.**

## 7. A Concrete Design for the First 90-Day Cycle

### The first three sessions, minute by minute

**Session 1: Orientation and Goal Setting (120 minutes)**

0:00-0:10 — Welcome, introductions (even though members know each other, state name, family, one hope for the group)

0:10-0:20 — Member agreement read aloud, signed by all (see next section)

0:20-0:30 — Confidentiality discussion: what it means, limits (harm to self/others), consequences of breach

0:30-0:50 — Joshua shares research findings (this report or summary): Why groups succeed, why they fail, what we're betting on

0:50-1:10 — Brainstorm: What is one shared 90-day goal we could take on together, where each of us owns one piece and we all win or lose based on collective delivery? (Examples: co-create a family summit, launch a joint philanthropic project, build a shared resource for next-gen, co-author a white paper)

1:10-1:30 — Vote on one goal; break it into five to seven pieces (one per member)

1:30-1:50 — Each member drafts their piece: what done looks like, weekly milestones, what help they need from the group

1:50-2:00 — Set weekly check-in cadence (15 minutes, same day/time, every week for 12 weeks)

**Session 2: Week 4 Check-In (60 minutes)**

0:00-0:05 — Restate the shared goal and each person's piece

0:05-0:35 — Round-robin (5 min each): What is on track? What is off track? What blockers? What help do you need?

0:35-0:50 — IDS (Identify-Discuss-Solve): Pick the biggest blocker. Discuss. Agree on next action.

0:50-0:60 — Confirm week 8 mid-cycle review date, adjourn

**Session 3: Week 8 Mid-Cycle Review (90 minutes)**

0:00-0:10 — Round-robin: Red/yellow/green status on your piece. If red, why?

0:10-0:40 — Problem-solving: If anyone is off track, what can the group do? Can we scope down? Can someone help? Is the goal still viable?

0:40-1:00 — Peer feedback: Each person gets 3 minutes to ask the group, "What am I missing? What should I consider?"

1:00-1:15 — Adjust the goal if necessary (this is allowed once per cycle)

1:15-1:30 — Plan week 12 showcase: How will we present what we built? Who is the audience (just us, or invite spouses/next-gen)?

**Weekly check-ins (weeks 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11): 15 minutes, asynchronous or synchronous**

- Each member posts or shares: % complete, biggest win this week, biggest blocker
- One person (rotating role) reads all updates and flags if anyone is stuck
- If stuck, schedule an extra 20-minute IDS call with that person + one other member

**Week 12: Showcase and Retrospective (120 minutes)**

0:00-0:10 — Welcome, restate the goal

0:10-0:50 — Each member presents their piece (5 min each + 2 min Q&A)

0:50-1:00 — Group reflection: Did we deliver the shared goal? (Yes/no/partially)

1:00-1:30 — Retrospective: What worked? What didn't? What surprised us? Would we do this again?

1:30-1:50 — Decide: Do we continue for another 90 days? Same structure or different? Do we formalize (LLC, co-op, or stay informal)?

1:50-2:00 — Deposit and surplus decision (if any revenue generated): Keep in reserve, split now, or other?

### Member Agreement in Plain Language

**Superachievers Member Agreement (Draft)**

We are [names], and we agree to the following for the next 90 days:

**1. What we are doing**

We are taking on one shared goal together: [goal]. Each of us owns one piece:
- [Member 1]: [piece]
- [Member 2]: [piece]
- [Member 3]: [piece]
- [Member 4]: [piece]
- [Member 5]: [piece]
- [Member 6]: [piece]
- [Member 7]: [piece] *(if seven members)*

We will meet three times (weeks 0, 4, 8, 12) and check in weekly.

**2. Confidentiality**

What is said in the group stays in the group. We will not share another member's challenges, progress, or personal information outside the group without their permission.

Exceptions:
- If we believe someone is at risk of harm to themselves or others, we will speak up.
- Spouses or family members who sit in on meetings are bound by the same rule.

If someone breaks confidentiality, we will discuss it as a group and decide whether they can continue.

**3. Showing up**

We commit to:
- Attend all four sessions (or send a family representative with our written update).
- Participate in weekly check-ins (15 minutes).
- Do our piece of the work.

**4. Finishing**

We will finish our piece by week 12. If we cannot, we will tell the group by week 8 so we can adjust.

If someone drops out without notice or misses two sessions in a row, their deposit is forfeit and their seat opens. The group votes on whether to continue with fewer members or invite someone new.

**5. Deposits and surplus**

Each member deposits [amount] at the start.

- If we complete the cycle and deliver the shared goal, deposits are refunded.
- If a member drops out or misses two sessions, their deposit stays in the group reserve.
- If the shared goal generates revenue or surplus, we will decide at week 12 how to split it (equal shares, patronage-based, or other).

**6. Selling to members**

We will not pitch products, services, or investments to other members during group time. Relationships outside the group are up to us, but inside the group, we are here to work together, not sell.

**7. New seats**

If a seat opens, the remaining members vote unanimously on whether to invite someone. If we cannot agree, the seat stays empty for this cycle.

**8. Family participation**

Each member may bring one family member (spouse, adult child, business partner) to sit in on one session per cycle as an observer. The observer signs a one-page confidentiality pledge and does not speak unless invited by the member.

**9. What happens if it doesn't work**

If at week 8 the group agrees the goal is not viable, we can:
- Pivot to a new goal (one pivot allowed per cycle)
- Complete individually and share results
- End early and return deposits

**10. Next cycle**

At week 12, we decide: continue, revise, or stop. No obligation beyond one cycle.

**Signed:**

- [Member 1], [Date]
- [Member 2], [Date]
- [Member 3], [Date]
- [Member 4], [Date]
- [Member 5], [Date]
- [Member 6], [Date]
- [Member 7], [Date] *(if seven members)*

### How to tell whether it worked

Measure at week 12:

**Outcome metrics:**
- Did we deliver the shared goal? (Yes / Partially / No)
- Did each member deliver their piece? (Count)

**Process metrics:**
- Did all members attend all four sessions? (Count absences)
- Did members complete weekly check-ins? (% compliance)
- Did anyone drop out? (Count)

**Satisfaction metrics (anonymous survey):**
- On a scale of 1-5, how valuable was this experience?
- Would you do another 90-day cycle with this group? (Yes / Maybe / No)
- What worked best?
- What would you change?
- Did you feel accountable to the group? (Yes / No)
- Did you trust the group's confidentiality? (Yes / No)

**Success thresholds:**
- **Good first cycle**: Goal delivered or partially delivered; no dropouts; 80%+ would do it again
- **Needs revision**: Goal not delivered but group still intact; 50-79% would do it again
- **Failed**: Dropouts, or <50% would do it again

If "Good," proceed to cycle 2 with same or adjusted structure.

If "Needs revision," hold a two-hour working session to redesign before committing to cycle 2.

If "Failed," end gracefully; distribute reserve if any; Joshua documents lessons.

## 8. Risks, and the Top 5 Questions Only Joshua Can Answer

### Risks

**1. Mismatch between what members want and what Superachievers delivers**

If people join expecting a trust-based sounding board and get a 90-day sprint with deliverables, they churn. If people join expecting a co-creation lab and get a talking circle, they leave. The concept is not wrong, but it is two things (peer support + shared execution), and that is inherently fragile.

**2. Dropout cascade**

In a five to seven person group with one shared goal, one person dropping out mid-cycle can break the whole project. Even with pieces scoped independently, morale drops when someone bails. If two people drop, the group may collapse entirely.

**3. Confidentiality breach in a small community**

If members know each other socially, or live in the same city, or their families overlap, leaks are more likely. One breach can destroy trust permanently in a small group.

**4. Family participation ambiguity**

"Someone from the family can sit in" is vague. If one member brings their spouse every time and another brings their COO once, those relationships are not equivalent. If spouses talk to each other outside the group, secondary confidentiality risk emerges.

**5. Surplus-sharing complexity before value is proven**

Designing credit and surplus-sharing structures before the group has completed one cycle is premature. If the group fails in cycle one, the legal structure is wasted cost.

**6. Over-designing the first cycle**

If the first 90 days are burdened with entity formation, governance documents, and legal fees, enthusiasm may die before work begins. Start simple.

**7. Scope creep on the shared goal**

A 90-day goal that requires coordination across five families' businesses, stakeholder buy-in, and external dependencies will almost certainly fail. The goal must be achievable by seven people working independently on their pieces with light coordination.

**8. Invitation-only with unanimous vote on new seats**

This is good for cohesion, bad for growth. If Superachievers is meant to scale beyond one group, the unanimous vote rule will bottleneck it. If it is meant to stay one group indefinitely, that is fine, but Joshua should decide now.

**9. Parked while Joshua tests alone**

If Superachievers stays parked through 2027, momentum dies. If Joshua wants to revive it, he must commit to a 2027 launch, even if small.

### Top 5 Questions Only Joshua Can Answer

**1. Is this a peer support group with optional shared projects, or a 90-day sprint group with peer support on the side?**

They are not the same. Peer forums meet monthly for years; 90-day sprints are intense, time-bound, and churn-heavy. Pick one for year one.

**Implications:**
- If peer support: Monthly meetings, multi-year commitment, shared goal is secondary or optional, deposits are lower or none, success is "would you recommend this group?"
- If 90-day sprint: Weekly check-ins, one cycle commitment, shared goal is primary, deposits are higher (skin in the game), success is "did we deliver?"

**2. What is the actual wealth and revenue threshold for membership?**

"Founders and family principals (solopreneurs welcome if they have real wealth and a family focus)" is undefined.

- Real wealth means what? $5M net worth? $20M? $100M?
- Does "family principals" mean single-family offices, or anyone with a family business?
- Does "family focus" mean they have kids, or they are thinking about legacy, or they employ family members?

TIGER 21 uses [$20M for standard membership, $200M for family office division](https://tiger21.com/membership-qualifications/). That clarity filters fast. Superachievers needs a number, or at least a range.

**Implications:**
- If the threshold is too low, the group may include people at very different life stages and resource levels, making peer advice less relevant.
- If the threshold is too high, the pool of candidates shrinks, especially if limiting to five to seven people Joshua knows personally.

**3. Who decides what the shared goal is each quarter?**

Is it Joshua? Unanimous vote? Majority? First person to propose a good idea?

If decision-making is unclear, the group will spend half of session 1 debating process instead of committing to work.

**Implications:**
- If Joshua decides: Faster, but members may feel less ownership.
- If unanimous vote: Slowest, highest buy-in, but one veto blocks progress.
- If majority: Faster than unanimous, but dissenting members may disengage.

**4. What happens to Superachievers if Joshua moves, gets busy, or loses interest?**

Is there a succession plan? Is someone else authorized to facilitate? Or does the group dissolve if Joshua steps back?

Groups that depend on the founder's energy often die when the founder's attention shifts. If Superachievers is meant to outlive Joshua's active involvement, design that in from the start (rotating facilitation, co-leadership, etc.).

**Implications:**
- If Joshua is essential: Superachievers is not a scalable model; it is a personal project.
- If the group can self-facilitate: Structure must support that (clear meeting agendas, rotating roles, documented process).

**5. Is this a U.S. group, a Mexico group, or both?**

If members are in both countries:
- Tax and legal complexity increases (cross-border LLC or co-op, treaty considerations, different surplus distribution rules).
- Time zones may not align for weekly check-ins.
- Cultural expectations around family, confidentiality, and formality may differ.

If it is U.S.-only or Mexico-only:
- Simpler legal and logistical structure.
- Easier to recruit from known networks.

**Implications:**
- If both: Budget for cross-border legal counsel from the start; expect slower decision-making.
- If one country only: Clarify that in outreach so people do not assume otherwise.
